# 10. No access control yet

Date: 2026-11-25

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

Cluebert has no accounts, logins or roles. Anyone who can reach the app can:

- start a game at `/`;
- run any game whose host URL they have (`/games/:id/host`) and watch any
  game whose player URL they have (`/games/:id`);
- create, rename, edit, move and delete collections and boards at `/boards`,
  and import boards at `/boards/import` (ADR 9).

The people using it are a host, usually a parent or teacher, and a room of
children aged 7 to 10 who watch a shared screen and have no devices of
their own (ADR 2). One person sets up and runs every game. Boards are
written by that person or generated by another agent and imported.

What the app stores:

- Boards: questions and answers written for lessons. Nothing personal.
- Team names, which may be children's first names. They are only ever held
  in memory, for the life of a game (ADR 4), and are never written to the
  database.

Game ids are 8 random URL-safe characters (48 bits), so a game's URLs can't
practically be guessed. But they are not secrets: they appear in the
browser's address bar and on the shared screen.

The app will run in an existing Kubernetes cluster (step 9 of the build
plan), alongside apps that are reachable from the internet through an
ingress with TLS. It is likely to be reachable the same way.

Adding access control now would mean choosing between, for example, HTTP
basic auth on the management pages, `phx.gen.auth` accounts, or an
identity-aware proxy in front of the cluster. Each brings secrets to manage,
a login step before every game, and, for accounts, a users table and more
pages. None of this protects anything that is at risk today.

## Decision

**There is no access control, for now.** No page asks who you are, and no
action checks it. This is a deliberate "you aren't gonna need it", not an
oversight.

The app must not grow features that would make this unsafe without
revisiting this decision first. In particular, nothing personal is
persisted, and no page exposes anything beyond boards and games.

## Consequences

- Playing needs no setup: open the app, add teams, start. Nothing to log in
  to on a classroom laptop.
- Anyone who can reach the app can change or delete every board, and can
  disrupt a game if they learn its host URL. On the internet, that includes
  strangers and crawlers. Boards can be re-imported from their JSON files,
  so the worst case is lost edits made in the app.
- Revisit this decision when any of these happen:
  - someone other than the host's household or class can reach the app and
    is not trusted with the boards, for example it is shared with other
    teachers or linked from somewhere public;
  - boards are lost or changed by someone who shouldn't have;
  - the app starts storing anything about people, such as saved scores,
    players or progress (ADR 4 already names saving final scores as a
    possible next step);
  - different people need different boards or collections of their own.
- The smallest next step would be to protect the management pages
  (`/boards` and below) only, with HTTP basic auth (`Plug.BasicAuth` with a
  password from the environment) or a basic-auth middleware at the ingress,
  leaving play open. Accounts would come only if people need their own
  boards.
